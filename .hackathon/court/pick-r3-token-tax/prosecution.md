# Prosecution — R3 token tax (N=5)

1. **Claim:** [V] Newer tokenizers have already cut most of the gap. o200k_base takes the mean Indic tax from 8.0x down to 2.1x, and Kannada down to 2.72x (arXiv 2607.24276, Section 6/Table 2; label taken from the case). A team fix measured against the old cl100k baseline would be solving a problem that is mostly gone.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A Kannada user of a current model already pays about 2.7x, not 12x. If the demo claims "₹ saved" against an outdated baseline, it overstates any real saving.
2. **Claim:** [AI] Indic model builders such as BharatGen PARAM-1 and Sarvam already extend tokenizers for Indian languages. The case says this comes from a snippet that was not opened, as does the "vocab extension 3.55 to 2.18" figure. Vocabulary extension is therefore a known, published fix, not a new one.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A Kannada app developer can already pick an Indic-tokenizer model today. A hackathon replication of known work gives them nothing new they can adopt.
3. **Claim:** [T] The team has no GPU laptops and about 21 hours. The case marks "can they train or evaluate anything meaningful on CPU in 24 h" as [?] unknown. Vocabulary extension needs continued training to re-learn the new embeddings. On CPU that will be tiny and short, and quality-loss numbers from it are unlikely to mean much.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A developer cannot trust a "tokens saved, small quality loss" result from an under-trained toy model. The failure analysis the rubric requires would be about too little training, not about Kannada.
4. **Claim:** [?] The paper did not measure latency, and the case records no assumption about it. The claim that users on slow mobile data "wait longer" is assumed, not shown. For the least-digital user, network round-trips and the size of the generated response may matter far more than input token count.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A Kannada-only user on 2G/3G may see no noticeable speed-up even if tokens drop. The fix could miss what they actually feel.
5. **Claim:** [AI] The cheaper option, a no-retrain transliteration or compression pre-pass, only treats the symptom. It works around the tokenizer instead of fixing it, and it can distort Kannada meaning, because transliterated or compressed text is not in the form the model learned. That risks wrong answers that neither the end user nor a filter can spot.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A Kannada speaker would get cheaper but quietly worse or wrong replies in their own language. That is a worse trade than paying more tokens for a correct answer.
