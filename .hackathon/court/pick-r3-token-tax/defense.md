# Defense — R3 token tax (N=5)

1. **Claim:** [V] The tax is large and measured: under cl100k_base, Kannada needs 12.06× as many tokens per word as English, and the mean across ten Indian languages is 8.0× (Srivastava, "The Tokenizer Tax", arXiv 2607.24276, 27 Jul 2026, Table 1; from the case).
   **Why it matters to a real user:** Every request a Kannada-speaking chatbot user sends is billed and generated as about 12× more tokens. They pay more, wait longer, and use more data on a slow mobile connection for the same message.
2. **Claim:** [V] The tax also cuts quality, not just cost. At a fixed 8,192-token budget, a Kannada user gets about 11.9% of the usable characters an English user gets, and even o200k_base still leaves Kannada at 2.72×. So the gap is smaller in newer tokenizers but not closed (same paper, Section 6 / Table 2).
   **Why it matters to a real user:** The developer building a Kannada app can fit roughly a ninth as much document, chat history or retrieved context. The app forgets earlier conversation or cuts off long Kannada documents sooner, and the end user sees that as worse answers.
3. **Claim:** [V] The proposed fix goes at the root cause, which is the vocabulary itself, not the prompt. Published work shows script-specific normalisation and Unigram or cluster-based vocabularies improve Indic tokenization (Brahma et al., arXiv 2506.17789, ACL 2026 Findings, abstract only). XLM-R's multilingual vocabulary already brings Kannada down to 1.86× (Srivastava, Table 2). The vocab-extension 3.55 → 2.18 figure is only [AI], snippet not opened.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A fix at the tokenizer level helps every Kannada request automatically. The end user does nothing and needs no new app. The operator swaps one component instead of rewriting prompts.
4. **Claim:** [V] The core measurement can be done without a GPU, so the "No GPU laptops" constraint [T] does not block the main result. SentencePiece trains Unigram/BPE directly from raw sentences in plain Python ([github.com/google/sentencepiece](https://github.com/google/sentencepiece), opened 2026-11-20). Counting tokens before and after, and converting that to ₹, is CPU arithmetic [AI]. Caveat: no published CPU training-time figure was found, and the continued-training and quality-loss part on CPU is still [?].
   **Why it matters to a real user:** The developer gets a reproducible table they can rerun on their own Kannada data on an ordinary laptop: tokens saved, ₹ saved, and the context gained.
5. **Claim:** [T] The track rules and the team's rough direction require a hypothesis, a baseline comparison against the stock tokenizer, a quality-loss measurement and a failure analysis, not just a claim of savings. Latency, which the paper did not measure [?], and where the fix fails are planned deliverables, not results yet.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A developer choosing a model or tokenizer for a Kannada app needs to know both what the fix saves and what it breaks (quality loss, code-mixed text, rare words). A tested trade-off they can trust is more useful than another bare compression number.
