# Prosecution — F1 irregular income (N=5)

1. **Claim:** [?] Nobody has shown how often a typical rider has a lean month or how big the dip is. The case lists both as unknown, and the team says [T] it has no real rider income data, so the backtest runs on synthetic income the team will make up itself.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A rider gets no proof the rule helps. A backtest on made-up income patterns shows only that the rule works on data built to suit it, not that it would have saved a real Bengaluru rider from a short week.
2. **Claim:** [V] About 40% of gig workers earn under ₹15,000 a month (Economic Survey 2025-26, via Upstox, 29 Jan 2026). Consistent delivery drivers net about ₹75 an hour (IDInsight 2024, via IDR, 20 Jan 2026). So the root cause looks like low income, not uneven timing.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** If a "good week" only just covers rent and fuel, there is nothing to put in a buffer. The self-salary just shows the shortfall without fixing it.
3. **Claim:** [V] When income fell, platform gig workers were more likely to borrow or sell assets than use savings (ADBI Working Paper 1495, Apr 2025, abstract). Whether riders would follow a buffer rule is [?] unknown. Also [T] the tool must not hold money, so the "buffer" is only a number on screen while the cash stays in the worker's pocket.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A rider being chased by a lender or with a family emergency can spend the "buffer" money with nothing to stop them. A rule they can ignore at no cost doesn't change the behaviour that leads to high-interest debt.
4. **Claim:** [T] The case names the least-digital user as an older Kannada-only street vendor with a basic phone and cash income. The direction relies on WhatsApp, which needs a smartphone, and on Kannada voice input, while cash earnings have to be logged by hand.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** The vendor is most exposed to swings in income but either can't use the tool or has to log every cash sale by voice each day. That is extra daily work, and the self-salary is only as good as what gets logged.
5. **Claim:** [AI] Budgeting and gig-worker finance apps already exist (the case labels this [AI], from snippets nobody opened). On top of that, [T] the team has about 21 hours and 4 people to build reliable Kannada speech recognition, WhatsApp integration, a lean-month predictor and a backtest, all without real data.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** An early warning fitted to synthetic data can be wrong: a false alarm makes a rider cut spending on food or fuel for no reason, and a missed warning leaves them with no time to prepare. Something rushed and untested in Kannada is worse for them than the tools they may already use.
