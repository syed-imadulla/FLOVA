# Defense — F1 irregular income (N=5)

1. **Claim:** [V] The problem is real and painful. When income fell, platform gig workers were more likely than non-platform workers to borrow or sell assets, and less likely to use savings (ADBI Working Paper 1495, Apr 2025, abstract only, as cited in the case). That is the failure the buffer rule is built to stop.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A Bengaluru rider who has no savings to fall back on in a bad week ends up taking costly loans or selling a phone or bike. A pre-filled buffer gives them something else to draw on.
2. **Claim:** [V] The margins are thin enough that one weak week hurts. About 40% of gig workers earn under ₹15,000 a month and many have thin credit files (Economic Survey 2025-26, via Upstox, 29 Jan 2026). Delivery drivers net only about ₹75/h after costs (IDInsight 2024, via IDR, 20 Jan 2026). So the cause is how income is timed, not only how much there is, and a self-salary rule targets the timing.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** A worker whose yearly income covers their needs but who has no credit to bridge a bad month gets a steady weekly amount. They don't have to plan spending around an unpredictable week.
3. **Claim:** [T] The plan reaches the least-digital user without an app. Input is by voice or WhatsApp in Kannada. The tool only sets a rule and sends warnings, so cash earners can report income by voice note on a basic or shared phone. A union, NGO or welfare-board worker could also enter it for them.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** An older street vendor who speaks only Kannada and is paid in cash can join by speaking. She doesn't need to read English screens or link a bank account.
4. **Claim:** [T] It is safe by design and needs no integration. The case says the tool must not hold money (it is regulated). So the core is a fixed, rule-based calculation (buffer target, weekly self-salary, lean-month trigger) that a person can check, not an opaque AI model. It sits next to the worker's existing cash, UPI or bank account with no regulated money flows.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** The worker keeps full control of their money. They can follow advice that is plain and explainable ("set aside ₹X this week"), and their funds are never put at risk.
5. **Claim:** [T]/[AI] One complete journey can be built and tested in about 21 h by a team of 4. [T] The journey is: log a week's income by voice, have the rule calculate this week's self-salary and buffer top-up, and send a lean-month warning. [T] Synthetic data is allowed if labelled. [AI] The team can backtest the rule against no rule on synthetic income patterns and report days short and debt avoided, which answers the rubric's Evidence (20) and difficult-cases (25) criteria directly. [?] The case does not claim this proves real-world uptake: how often lean months hit and whether riders will follow the rule are both still unknown.
   **Why it matters to a real user:** Unions and welfare boards get a clear, testable rule they could pilot with riders. The case's [AI] note says existing budgeting apps lack this: no independent evaluation found that they improve outcomes.
